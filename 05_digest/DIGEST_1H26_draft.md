## What changed in 1H26

1. All three lost about 3% NII YoY on lower SORA and SOFR. Volume growth (average assets up 5% to 12%) and hedging cushioned it. None of them could fully offset the margin decline through the balance sheet.
2. Non-interest income carried the half. DBS fee income +20%, OCBC +26%, both on wealth. UOB fees flat because loan and trade fees fell while wealth and cards rose. The divergence between UOB and the other two is the single most important competitive fact in the period.
3. Wealth AUM crossed thresholds: DBS 516 SGD b (half-trillion), OCBC 350 SGD b. Net new money DBS 21 SGD b in six months versus UOB 4 SGD b.
4. Cost discipline diverged. OCBC let opex run +10% (performance pay, tech) while still improving CIR because income grew faster. UOB held opex to +2% but CIR still worsened to 44.9 because income fell. DBS in between at +4%.
5. Asset quality: DBS and OCBC stable at 1.0 and 0.9. UOB NPL rose to 1.6 on a single Greater China real-estate downgrade; Greater China NPL 4.8%.
6. Capital return is now a differentiator. DBS pays a 15c per quarter capital return dividend on top of ordinary. OCBC is completing S$2.5b. UOB is 40% through a S$2b buyback. All three are running down surplus CET1 deliberately.
7. Guidance: DBS FY26 net profit slightly below 2025; UOB cut fee growth guidance to low single digit and holds NIM at 1.75 to 1.80; OCBC gave no numeric update in the release.
8. Tech: UOB disclosed 30,000 staff on Microsoft Copilot with 300+ use cases. DBS was asked about its AI economic value target on the call. Track both as the "AI disclosure" row.

## Preliminary implications for Maybank (to be tested against Maybank 1H26 results)

- Margin: if Maybank's NIM held better than the SG three, the story is funding mix and MYR rate environment, not franchise strength. Separate the two in the card.
- Wealth: SG banks converted the rate downturn into a fee upcycle. The Maybank question is whether Group Wealth income growth is in the same range (+16% to +39%) and whether AUM net new money is disclosed at all. If Maybank does not disclose NNM, that is itself a gap to raise.
- Cost: OCBC's willingness to spend +10% while improving CIR is the counter-example to a flat-cost mandate. The lever is income growth, not cost cutting.
- Capital: all three SG banks are returning surplus capital explicitly. Maybank's payout and DRP policy will be compared against this by investors.
- Singapore market: Maybank Singapore segment results allow a like-for-like comparison against the SG three's domestic books, and against SCBSL and HSBCSG once their statements are loaded.
